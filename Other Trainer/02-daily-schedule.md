# 02 — Daily schedule

## Total budget, not per-module budgets

The scheduler starts from **one number: minutes available today.** It then allocates across enabled modules. Modules do not own time; the day does.

This is the design that survives real life. Per-module budgets mean a 50-minute obligation that either happens entirely or not at all. A total budget with allocation means a 20-minute day is a *complete* day with less in it, rather than a failure.

## Default composition

| Slot | Minutes | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Korean | 18 | Its own internal blocks (`korean-trainer/specs/03-session-flow.md`) |
| Knowledge primary | 15 | One domain, rotating monthly |
| Knowledge SRS | 5 | Mixed across all four domains |
| Chess | 10 | When merged |

## Reduced days

The learner sets today's budget — or the app infers it from the hour. Allocation priority when the budget is short:

1. **All modules' due SRS**, proportionally — retention first, always
2. Korean new material — it is on a fixed two-year clock
3. Knowledge primary block
4. Chess

A 10-minute day is due cards only, across everything. It counts fully.

## One streak

Platform-wide. A day counts if any module completed its due queue. Never per-module streaks — four streaks is four ways to feel bad and four independent reasons to quit.

One grace day per week, applied silently.

## The arithmetic worth looking at before enabling everything

Full composition is **48 minutes a day** of focused practice. Plus the Korean ambient listening (~25 min in dead time) and home speaking (~10 min, embedded in the evening). Call it an hour a day of deliberate self-improvement alongside several ventures and a young family.

That is achievable. It is also more than it sounds, and the characteristic failure is not dropping one module — it is all of them degrading together into something done inattentively.

**So stagger the starts.** Korean alone until it has 60 days of adherence data, because it has the hardest adherence requirement and a fixed clock. Then add the knowledge module and watch what happens to the Korean numbers. If they hold, add the next.

The platform is built to make this easy: enabling a module is a manifest flag, and the schedule absorbs it. Enable them one at a time and let the adherence data decide, rather than deciding now.
