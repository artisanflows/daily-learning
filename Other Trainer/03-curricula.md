# 03 — The four spines

Each is ~180 sessions of primary time over two years (6 months × ~30 days), plus continuous SRS.

## 1. Physics — conceptual, no problem sets

The goal is following an argument, not solving for x. That is a legitimate target, but it constrains the route: **the sequence must be conceptually honest, or it becomes physics appreciation with holes.**

| Block | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | Motion, force, momentum — the Newtonian frame and what it assumes |
| 2 | **Energy and conservation** — the single biggest idea in physics; symmetry and conservation |
| 3 | Fields: gravitational, then electric and magnetic |
| 4 | **Waves, superposition, interference** |
| 5 | Thermodynamics, entropy, the statistical picture |
| 6 | Electromagnetism unified; light as an EM wave; the constancy of *c* falls out |
| 7 | **Special relativity** — derived from block 6, not from trains |
| 8 | General relativity: equivalence principle → curvature |
| 9 | Blackbody and photoelectric → quantisation |
| 10 | Wave-particle duality, superposition, the measurement problem |
| 11 | Entanglement and Bell's theorem — done properly, including what it forbids |
| 12 | Standard Model sketch; open problems |

Two sequencing decisions that matter:

**Waves before quantum mechanics (block 4 before 10).** Most popular treatments skip this, and it is the main reason people never actually grasp superposition — they meet it first as a quantum mystery rather than as something water and sound already do.

**Special relativity out of electromagnetism (block 6 → 7).** The historically and conceptually honest route: Maxwell's equations give a fixed speed of light, and relativity is what you get by taking that seriously. Thought experiments illustrate; they do not motivate.

Interleave the misconception cards from `02-content-pipeline.md` §3 throughout.

## 2. Wine — modular by country and grape

### The organising principle differs by country, and that is lesson one

This is the structural insight the module is built on. Each wine country answers "what does the label tell you?" differently, and learning *that* is worth more than any individual appellation:

| Country | Organised by | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **France** | Place (AOC). Grape usually absent from the label. | Blending is the norm — Bordeaux (Cab Sauv / Merlot / Cab Franc / Petit Verdot / Malbec), Rhône (GSM), Champagne (Chard / PN / Meunier). Burgundy is the exception: monovarietal but still place-labelled. |
| **Italy** | Place + native grape, DOC/DOCG | Enormous native varietal diversity. Brunello di Montalcino is 100% Sangiovese; Barolo and Barbaresco 100% Nebbiolo; Chianti Classico min. 80% Sangiovese. |
| **Spain** | Grape + **time in oak and bottle** | Joven / Crianza / Reserva / Gran Reserva is an ageing classification, not a quality region — genuinely distinctive and widely misread. |
| **Germany** | Grape + **ripeness at harvest** | Kabinett → Spätlese → Auslese → BA → TBA measures must weight, not sweetness directly. VDP's Grosses Gewächs adds a newer place-based layer. |
| **New World** | Grape first, region second | Varietal labelling. The inverse of France. |

Each country module opens by teaching its organising principle, then works through regions inside it. That is why the modules genuinely differ in shape rather than being the same template with different names.

### Sequence

| Block | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Systematic tasting** — fixed structure: acid, tannin, alcohol, body, fruit, finish |
| 2 | The ~15 grapes that carry most of the world — characteristics, typical expressions, food pairing logic |
| 3 | Winemaking, and how each decision shows in the glass |
| 4 | **France** — Bordeaux, Burgundy, Rhône, Loire, Champagne, Alsace |
| 5 | **Italy** — Piedmont, Tuscany, Veneto, the south |
| 6 | **Spain and Portugal** |
| 7 | **Germany and Austria** |
| 8 | **New World** — California, Oregon, Australia, NZ, Chile, Argentina, South Africa |
| 9 | Sparkling and fortified in depth |
| 10 | Ageing, storage, faults, vintage variation |

Block 1 first and permanently: without a tasting structure every subsequent bottle is an anecdote rather than a data point.

**Granularity works.** A single appellation is a clean session — Brunello di Montalcino in one sitting covers 100% Sangiovese, the Montalcino mesoclimate being warmer and drier than Chianti, the five-year minimum ageing with two in oak, six for Riserva, and how that shows in the glass. An "introduction to Italian wine regions" is one overview session before the region-by-region ones. The atomic unit is a concept or an appellation, and both naturally fit the block.

**Pairing** is taught as logic rather than lists — match acid to fat, tannin to protein, sweetness above the dish, weight to weight — then instantiated per grape in block 2.

**One bottle weekly with a structured note, from week one**, regardless of the current primary domain.

## 3. Art — 200 artists, two layers

Two layers that do different jobs, both worth having:

**Recognition layer — the 200.** For each artist: who they were, when and where, 2–4 canonical works with correct titles, the movement they sit in, and one sentence on why they matter. This is excellent SRS material — arguably the best in the whole app, because it is genuinely fact-shaped and image-driven. Roughly 200 artists over two years is a comfortable pace.

Card shape: **image → artist, period, and one reason it matters.** The image comes first; the point is to recognise unseen work, not to recall a name from a name.

**Understanding layer — one work per session** in the primary block, with formal analysis and the closing question *what problem was this artist solving?* This is what stops the 200 from being a very well-drilled quiz.

The recognition layer is what you asked for and it earns its place. The understanding layer is what makes the recognition mean something — knowing that Velázquez painted Las Meninas is a fact; knowing what it does with the viewer's position is the thing worth two years.

### Sequence

| Block | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Ways of looking** — composition, line, colour, light, space, facture |
| 2 | Ancient and classical |
| 3 | Medieval, Byzantine, early Renaissance |
| 4 | High Renaissance and Mannerism |
| 5 | Baroque and Dutch Golden Age |
| 6 | Neoclassicism, Romanticism, Realism |
| 7 | Impressionism and Post-Impressionism |
| 8 | Modernism: Cubism through Abstract Expressionism |
| 9 | Post-war and contemporary |
| 10 | Interleaved throughout: Islamic, Chinese, Japanese, African, Mesoamerican |

Block 1 before any chronology — without formal vocabulary the survey is a list of names attached to thumbnails.

Images from Met and Rijksmuseum open access (`02-content-pipeline.md` §2). Non-Western material interleaved rather than appended; appending it structurally implies it is a footnote.

**Monthly museum visit**, three works chosen in advance.

## 4. Psychology — methodology first

| Block | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Methods and the replication crisis** — effect sizes, power, p-values, preregistration, publication bias, garden of forking paths |
| 2 | Perception and attention |
| 3 | Memory |
| 4 | Learning and conditioning |
| 5 | Cognition, heuristics and biases — with honest effect sizes |
| 6 | Development |
| 7 | Social psychology — the most heavily caveated block |
| 8 | Personality and individual differences (Big Five, behavioural genetics) |
| 9 | Psychopathology and clinical |
| 10 | Neuroscience foundations |

**Block 1 is not preliminary throat-clearing; it is the most important block in the entire app.** Psychology is the one domain here where the widely available material is substantially unreliable, and a trainer that drills it would leave you confidently wrong. Learning to read an effect size and ask whether something replicated is more valuable than any individual finding, and it transfers to everything else you read.

Every finding thereafter carries its replication status tag (`02-content-pipeline.md` §3). Where a famous result failed, teach the failure — the story of ego depletion or power posing is more instructive than the finding ever was.
